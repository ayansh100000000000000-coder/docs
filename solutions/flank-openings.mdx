These are the solutions to [Chapter 4](/puzzles/flank-openings) of _The Permanent Advantage_. One of these puzzles shows a move that was not the best choice; for that one, the solution also shows a better option. The ratings are teaching judgments from opening theory and game commentary, not engine evaluations.

## Puzzle 17: Réti vs Bogoljubov

New York tournament, 1924<br /> Réti Opening. Result: 1-0 (25 moves)

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 12.f3!** Good move (pawn break)
</Check>

**Game line:** 12.f3 exf3 13.Bxf3 Qc7 14.Nxd7 Bxd7 15.e4 e5 16.c5

The pawn on e4 cramps White and blocks the g2 bishop. White hits it at once with f3. After the exchange the bishop sees the whole long diagonal, the f-file opens for the rook, and White can follow up with e4 to take over the centre. Réti's flank opening worked exactly as intended: he attacked Black's centre from the side instead of occupying it early.

## Puzzle 18: Capablanca vs Tartakower

New York tournament, 1924<br /> Dutch Defence. Result: 1-0 (52 moves)

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 9...Ne4** Good move (thematic)
</Check>

**Game line:** 9...Ne4 10.Bxe7 Nxc3 11.bxc3 Qxe7 12.a4 Bxf3 13.Qxf3 Nc6

The knight jumps to e4, the classic Dutch outpost supported by the f5 pawn. It frees Black's position through exchanges and leaves White with doubled c-pawns. The long-term problem came later: after 12...Bxf3 Black gave up the light-squared bishop, and Capablanca slowly exploited the weak light squares. He won the famous rook endgame that followed.

## Puzzle 19: Larsen vs Spassky

USSR vs Rest of the World, Belgrade, 1970<br /> Nimzo-Larsen Attack. Result: 0-1 (17 moves)

<Danger>
  **Played in the game: 10.f4?** Mistake
</Danger>

**Game line:** 10.f4 Ng4 11.g3 h5 12.h3 h4 13.hxg4 hxg3 14.Rg1 Rh1! 15.Rxh1 g2 16.Rf1 Qh4+ 17.Kd1 gxf1=Q+

White wants to support the centre, but f4 weakens the e3 pawn and the dark squares around the king while White is still undeveloped. Spassky's knight jumped to g4 at once, and the h-pawn and rook sacrifice tore the kingside open. A calmer developing move such as 10.Nc3 keeps the position solid and finishes development first.

## Puzzle 20: Spassky vs Fischer

World Championship, Game 3, Reykjavik, July 1972<br /> Modern Benoni. Result: 0-1 (41 moves)

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 11...Nh5!?** Good move (bold)
</Check>

**Game line:** 11...Nh5 12.Bxh5 gxh5 13.Nc4 Ne5 14.Ne3 Qh4

Black offers the knight for the e2 bishop. After 12.Bxh5 gxh5 Black's kingside pawns are broken, but Black gets the bishop pair, control of the dark squares and a powerful knight on e5. Fischer judged that these active pieces matter more than a damaged structure. It was his first ever win against Spassky and turned the match around.
