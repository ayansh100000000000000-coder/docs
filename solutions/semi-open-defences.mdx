These are the solutions to [Chapter 3](/puzzles/semi-open-defences) of _The Permanent Advantage_. Each one shows the move played in the game, a rating, the game line that followed and the positional idea behind it. The ratings are teaching judgments from opening theory and game commentary, not engine evaluations.

## Puzzle 12: Nimzowitsch vs Salwe

Karlsbad tournament, 1911<br /> French Defence, Advance Variation. Result: 1-0

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 7.dxc5!** Good move (instructive)
</Check>

**Game line:** 7.dxc5 Bxc5 8.O-O f6 9.b4 Be7 10.Bf4

White gives up the d4 pawn, which looks like surrendering the centre. The real goal is the d4 square itself. With the pawn gone, White can put pieces on d4, where they blockade Black's d-pawn and can never be chased away by a pawn. Nimzowitsch made this game the model example of blockade in _My System_.

## Puzzle 13: Fischer vs Petrosian

Candidates Final, Game 7, Buenos Aires, October 1971<br /> Sicilian Defence, Kan Variation. Result: 1-0

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 6.Nxc6** Good move (clear plan)
</Check>

**Game line:** 6.Nxc6 bxc6 7.O-O d5 8.c4 Nf6 9.cxd5 cxd5 10.exd5 exd5

The exchange pulls Black's b-pawn to c6. When White follows up with c4 and opens the centre, Black ends up with an isolated d-pawn and weak dark squares such as c5. Fischer then traded down into an endgame and later played the famous 22.Nxd7+, giving up his good knight to remove Black's best defender.

## Puzzle 14: Karpov vs Korchnoi

Candidates Final, Game 2, Moscow, 1974<br /> Sicilian Defence, Dragon Variation, Yugoslav Attack. Result: 1-0

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 13.Bxc4** Good move (thematic)
</Check>

**Game line:** 13.Bxc4 Rxc4 14.h5 Nxh5 15.g4 Nf6 16.Nde2 Qa5 17.Bh6

The knight on c4 hits the queen and the e3 bishop and is Black's best attacking piece. White removes it at once. While Black recaptures with the rook, White gains the time to push h5 and open the h-file against Black's king. In the Yugoslav Attack, trading off Black's queenside attackers while keeping your own kingside attack is the central idea.

## Puzzle 15: Karpov vs Kasparov

World Championship, Game 16, Moscow, October 1985<br /> Sicilian Defence, Taimanov Variation. Result: 0-1 (40 moves)

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 8...d5!?** Good move (bold gambit)
</Check>

**Game line:** 8...d5 9.cxd5 exd5 10.exd5 Nb4 11.Be2 Bc5 12.O-O O-O

Black gives a pawn to free the position at once and exploit the misplaced knight on a3. The knight lands on b4 and later on d3, where it cramps White's whole position. This idea is now called the Kasparov Gambit. Later analysis found improvements for White, so it is a bold practical choice rather than a refutation of White's setup.

## Puzzle 16: Short vs Timman

Tilburg, 1991<br /> Alekhine Defence, Modern Variation. Result: 1-0 (34 moves)

<Check>
  **Played in the game: 10.a4** Good move (prophylactic)
</Check>

**Game line:** 10.a4 dxe5 11.dxe5 Nd4 12.Nxd4 Qxd4 13.Re1

Black threatened ...a4, chasing the b3 bishop and gaining space on the queenside. White stops this for good and fixes the a5 pawn. The bishop keeps its strong diagonal, and the space-gaining pawn on e5 stays secure. This game is famous for its finish, when Short's king walked from g1 to g5 to help deliver mate.
